\section{Abstract}
%sam did this
Strong gravitational lensing of gravitational waves is an expected consequence of the propagation of compact binary signals through an inhomogeneous universe, and a small but non-zero fraction of events detected by ground based observatories should be multiply imaged by intervening galaxies or clusters \cite{2}\cite{6}\cite{18}\cite{19}.
Recent forecasts indicate that the first confident strongly lensed gravitational wave detection could occur during the fifth LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA observing run (O5)\cite{19}, while searches incorporating O1-O4a events forecast a substantial probability of reaching at least $3\sigma$ significance in O5\cite{20}.
At the same time, simulations tailored to the wide field capabilities of the Euclid telescope demonstrate that high resolution imaging can successfully indentify and localize the strongly lensed host galaxies of merging binary black holes, significantly narrowing down the gravitatioanl wave sky localization area \cite{27}.
Motivated by these results, this work develops a Bayesian framework in which a catalogued lens with resolved strong lensing information is used to increase the confidence of a candidate lensed GW pair.
We derive the full resolved strong lensing evidence, reconstruct the binary black hole population for prior odds, gravitational wave only bayes factor and joint analysis of the evidence, producing a joint bayes factor. The remaining hypotheses describe a catalogued lens without resolved images, an uncatalogued lens and two independent unlensed binary black holes.
The central forecast to be tested is that targeted (EM) follow-up can, for favourable resolved strong lensing systems, sharpen the lens prediction sufficiently to raise the strong lensing detection confidence toward $5\sigma$.\\
Keywords: gravitational waves; gravitational lensing: strong; Bayesian inference; electromagnetic catalogues; binary black holes; multi-messenger astronomy.

\section{Introduction}
%sam did this
Gravitational waves propagate along null geodiscs and are therefore subject to gravitatioanl lensing in the sam manner as electromagnetic radiation in the geometric optics limit \cite{1}\cite{2}. 
For galaxy scale lenses, a single compact binary coalescence can produce multiple detectable images separated by minutes to months, depending on the lens potential and source geometry. 
To leasing order, the images share the same intrinsic binary paramters while differing in arrival time, amplitude through the lensing magnification, and an image type dependent phase contribution \cite{3}\cite{4}\cite{5}\cite{6}. 
This repeated signal structure motivates searches for pairs or multiples of GW events that are mutually consistent under a common source hypothesis.\\
\\The central statistical difficulty is that an unlensed population can also produce event pairs with overlapping posterior distibutions.
A robust lensing claim must therefore compare a common source moel against a model in which the detections arise from independent binaries.
Bayesian model selection provides a natural framework because it combines likelihood agreement, prior information, population expectations and parameter space volume into a single evidence ratio \cite{7}\cite{8}\cite{9}.
Early work proposed posterior overlap statistics for lensed GW indentification \cite{3}, while subsequent analyses incorporated lensing time-delay distributions, astrophysical populations, selections effects and full join parameter estimation \cite{7}\cite{8}\cite{9}\cite{10}.
Searches of LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA data have so far found no compelling population level evidence for strong lensing, reinforcing the need for careful prior odds and follow up tests \cite{12}\cite{13}\cite{14}.\\
\\A major source source of additional information is the electromagnetic foreground.
A galaxy or cluster capable of producing strong lensing may be visable in wide field imaging or spectroscopic catalogues even when the binary black hole (BBH) source itself has no EM counterpart.
The EM data may range from a simple galaxy detection with photometry and redshift information to a resolved strong lensing system with arcs or multiple images of an unrelated background source.
Such data can constrain the lens mass distribution, position, redshift and velocity scale, and therefore predict distributions for the same lensing observables, such as relative time delay and magnification ratio, that are inferred from the GW pair.
Recent work on lens reconstruction and catalogue informed priors further demonstrates that EM information can break degeneracies that remain in GW only analyses and can materially alter lensing odds \cite{15}\cite{16}\cite{17}.

\section{Bayesian hypothesis space}
%sams part
Consider two GW detections, $d_{GW}={d_{GW,1},d_{GW,2}}$, together with EM observations dEM drawin from a catalogue of potential foreground lenses. 
We divide the lensing interpretation into three mutually exclusive observational cases and compare their union with an unlensed alternative.
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Mutually exclusive hypotheses used in the joint GW-EM model comparison}
\label{}
\begin{tabular}{|p{2cm}|p{3cm}|p{4cm}|}
\hline
Hypothesis & GW interpretation & EM/catalogue information \\ \hline
$H_1$       & Two detections are lensed images of the same BBH.        & The repsonsible lens is catalogues and resolved strong-lensing EM information constrains a detailed lens model.         \\ \hline
$H_2$       & Two detections are lensed images of the same BBH.        & The responsible lens is catalogues, but no resolved strong-lensing image configuration is available; EM data constrain galaxy properties.        \\ \hline
$H_3$       & Two detections are lensed images of the same BBH.        & The responsible foreground lens is absent from the catalogue.              \\ \hline
$H_U$       & The detections arise from two independent unlensed BBHs.         & Any galaxy in the search region is treated as an unrelated field object, conditional on the search selection.              \\ \hline  
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
\begin{equation}
H_L = H_1 \cup H_2 \cup H_3
\end{equation}
with $H_1$, $H_2$ and $H_3$ mutually exclusive within the assumed search space. 
The posterior probability of the total lensed hypothesis is therefore
\begin{equation}
p(H_L|d) = p(H_1|d) + p(H_2|d) + p(H_3|d)
\end{equation}
where $d={d_{GW},d_{EM}}$. For each model $H_i$, Bayes' theorem gives
\begin{equation}
p(H_i|d) = Z_i \frac{p(H_i)}{p(d)}, Z_i \equiv p(d|H_i).
\end{equation}
The posterior odds of lensing relative to the unlsened hypothesis are consequently
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:posteriorodds}
\frac{p(H_L|d)}{p(H_U|d)} = \frac{[Z_1p(H_1) + Z_2p(H_2) + Z_3p(H_3)]}{Z_Up(H_U)}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:posteriorodds} makes the role of the hypothesis prior explicit. 
The Bayes factor quantifies how the data update the relative support for the models, while the prior odds encode the expected rarity of strong lensing and the probability that a responsible lens belongs to each catalogue class. 
This distinction is important because prior odds and search selection can change substantially as the GW catalogue grows \cite{7}\cite{9}\cite{14}.

\section{Parameterisation and Conditional Structure}
\subsection{Binary Black Hole Source Parameters}
Let $\theta_{BBH}$ denote the physical parameters of the compact binary source.
A representative set is
\begin{equation}
\theta_{BBH}=\{m_1,m_2,\chi_1,\chi_2,z_s,\Omega_s,\iota,\phi_{pol},...\}.
\end{equation}
The exact waveform parameterisation mat instead use detector frame masses, luminosity distance and spin coordinates.
Under any lensed hypothesis, the intrinsic source parameters are shared by the images, whereas under $H_U$ the two detections have independent parameter sets $\theta_{BBH,1}$ and $\theta_{BBH,2}$.
Apparent luminosity distances are image dependent because magnification rescales the observed strain amplitude.

\subsection{Lens and Source Plane Parameters}
The lens is described by a model dependent parameter vector
\begin{equation}
\theta_{lens}=\{z_l,\sigma_v,q,\phi_l,\Omega_l,\gamma_{ext},\phi_{ext},...\}
\end{equation}
where $z_l$ is the lens redshift, $\sigma_v$ s velocity dispersion or equivalent madd scale parameter, $q$ the projected axis ratio, $\phi_l$ the lens orientation, $\Omega_l$ the lens position, and $\gamma_{ext}$ and $\phi_{ext}$ describe external shear.
The source position $\beta$ relative to the caustic must also be included.

\subsection{Image Level Lensing Observables}
The GW pair constrains a lower dimensional set of relations between the images.
We denote these by
\begin{equation}
\psi_{GW}=\{\Delta t, \mu_r, \Delta n, \Omega, ...\}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t$ is the relative arrive time delay, $\mu_r$ is a relative magnification ratio, $\Delta n$ denotes the relative mMorse index, and $\Omega$ represents common sky localisation.
These quantities are not independent free parameters in a physical lens model; they are generated by the lens equation and Fermat potenital,
\begin{equation}
\psi=f(\theta_{lens},z_s,\beta;cosmology).
\end{equation}
This mapping is the bridge between the GW and EM analyses: the GW data constrain $\psi$ directly or semi-directly, while EM observations constrain $\theta_{lens}$ and thereby induce a predictive distribution for $\psi$.

\subsection{Electromagnetic Nuisance Parameters}
EM observations require nuisance parameters $\nu_{EM}$ describing measurement and modelling uncertainties.
Depending on the catalogue and imaging data, $\nu_{EM}$ may include the point spread function, photometruc calibration, specral energy distribution parameters, stellar mass to light ratio, background subtraction, photometric redshift calibration and other instrument or population level terms.
These parameter should be maginalised rather than fixed when their uncertainty is non negligible, beause overly narrow EM constraints can artificially increase the GW-EM consistency factor.

\section{Unlensed Hypothesis}
%sam
Under $H_U$ the detections correspond to two independent BBH mergers.
The GW evidence is
\begin{equation}
Z_{GW,U} = \int p(d_{GW,1}|\theta_{BBH,1},H_U) p(d_{GW,2}|\theta_{BBH,2},H_U) \pi_U(\theta_{BBH,1},\theta_{BBH,2})d\theta_{BBH,1}d\theta_{BBH,2}
\end{equation}
The EM observations must not be allowed to depend pn whether the GW pair happens to be unlensed.
We therefore introduce an EM field model, $H_{EM,field}$, that describes unrelated foreground galaxies under the same catalogue/search selection.
When the GW and EM data are conditionally independent under $H_U$,
\begin{equation}
H_U \equiv H_{GW,U} \cap H_{EM,field}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
Z_U = Z_{GW,U} Z_{EM,field}, Z_{EM,field} = p(d_{EM}|H_{EM,field})
\end{equation}
This term is essential whenever the lensed hypotheses recieve evidence from the existence or properties of a catalogue galaxy.
Omitting the corresponding field galaxy probability from $H_U$ would systematically favour catalogues lens hypotheses simply because a galaxy was searched for and found.

\Theta_1 = \{\theta_{BBH},\theta_{lens},\beta_{GW},\theta_{EMsrc},\beta_{EMsrc},\nu_{EM}\}
\end{equation}
The parameters $\Theta_{BBH}$ describe the common BBH source, $\theta_{lens}$ describes the foreground mass model, and $\beta_{GW}$ is the BBH source plane position.
The parameters $\theta_{EMsrc}$ and $\beta_{EMsrc}$ describe the independently lansed background EM source, while $\nu_{EM}$ contains observational nuisance parameters such as the point spread function, calibration, sky background and noise model.
The central coupling is that the same $\theta_{lens}$ appears in both the GW and EM likelihoods.\\

\begin{equation}
p(d_{GW},d_{EM,c}|\Theta_1,H_1) = p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{lens},\beta_{GW},H_1)p(d_{EM,c}|\theta_{lens},\theta_{EMsrc},\beta_{EMsrc},\nu_{EM},H_1)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Full joint evidence and prior}
%sam
For a catalogue candidate c, the primary model selection quantity is the full joint evidence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H1evidence}
Z_{1,c} = \int p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{lens},\beta_{GW},H_1)p(d_{EM,c}|\theta_{lens},\theta_{EMsrc},\beta_{EMsrc},\nu_{EM},H_1)\pi_1(\Theta_1)d\Theta_1
\end{equation}
A useful schematic prior factorisation is
\begin{equation}
\pi_1(\Theta_1)=\pi_{BBH}\pi_{lens}\pi_{\beta,GW}\pi_{\nu,EM}S_1
\end{equation}
where $S_1$ denotes the required GW detection, catalogue and resolved lens selection conditioning.
This factorisation is schematic: correlations implied by a population model or a particular lens modelling analysis should be retained rather than forced into independent factors.
The evidence integrates over both scientifically interesting and nuisance parameters. Consequently, uncertainties in the resolved EM source model and image reconstruction contribute to the posterior volume and cannot in general be removed by fixing them at their maximum likelihood values.
Formulations of the full joint evidence for $H_2$ and $H_3$ can be found in the appendix.

\subsection{GW observables and the EM predictive distribution}
The GW pair constrains a reduced set of lensing observables
\begin{equation}
\psi = \{\Delta t,\mu_r,\Delta n,\Omega,...\}
\end{equation}
giving a posterior $p(\psi|d_{GW},H_1)$.
The resolved EM observations instead constrain the lens potential.
Propagating the EM constrained lens posterior through the lens equation gives a predictive distribution for the GW observables:
\begin{equation}
p(\psi|d_{EM,c},H_1) = \int p(\psi|\theta_{lens},z_s,\beta_{GW},H_1)p(\theta_{lens},z_s,\beta_{GW}|d_{EM,c},H_1)d\theta_{lens}dz_sd\beta_{GW}
\end{equation}
The BBH source redshift and source plane position remain latentunless independent measured.
The resolved EM source may occupy a different source plane position and have different redshift; its role is to constrain $\theta_{lens}$, which is then used to predict the lensing observables of the GW source.\\
\\The corresponding prior corrected consistency statistic is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H1consistency}
C_1 = \int [\frac{p(\psi|d_{GW}H_1)p(\psi|d_{EM,c},H_1)}{\pi(\psi|H_1)}]d\psi
\end{equation}
A large $C_1$ indicates that the resolved EM reconstruction predicts time delays, magnification relations and image properties compatible with the GW pair.
A small value indicates tension.
When the full evidence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H1evidence} is evaluated directly, this consistency is already encoded through the shared lens parameters; $C_1$ is therefore most safely interpreted as a reduced space diagnostic unless an explicit non overlapping factorisation is derived.

\subsection{Multiple resolved lens candidates}
If more than one resolved strong lensing system is compatiable with the GW localisation, the lens indentity should be marginalised rather than fixed in advance:
\begin{equation}
Z_1 = \sum_{c=1}^{N_{cat}}p(c|H_1)Z_{1,c}
\end{equation}

\section{Reduced GW-EM consistency under $H_1$}

\subsection{Prior corrected posterior overlap}
The reduced consistency statistic defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H1consistency} isolates the part of the inference that directly compares the GW inferred image relation with the prediction of the resolved EM lens reconstruction.
Its prior denominator removes the common prior contribution that would otherwise be counted twice when multiplying posteriors.\\
\\For $H_1$, this diagnostic is particularly informative bevause the resolved strong lensing configuration can make $p(\psi|d_{EM},H_1)$ substantially narrower than the corresponding $H_2$ distribution.
The same EM precision therefore produces stronger discrimination: agreement can yield a concentrated overlap, while a narrow but displaced prediction can suppress $C_1$ rapidly.

\subsection{Relation to the full evidence}
The full evidence $Z_1$ remains the reference model selection quantity.
The overlap $C_1$ should not be multiplied onto an already complete evaluation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H1evidence} unless the likelihood and prior structure has been explicitly rearranged into non overlapping factors.
In a direct joint calculation, GW-EM compatibility is already present because $\theta_{lens}$ is shared between both likelihoods.

\section{Computational evaluation of $H_1$}
The integrals can be manipulated and approximated using laplace to get a form more suitable for computational analysis.
Using the joint maximum and laplace approximation the full evidence for $H_1$ can be written, in log form as,
\begin{equation}
\label{laplaceH1evidence}
\ln(Z_{1,c}) \approx \ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)+(\frac{k_1}{2})\ln(2\pi)-(\frac{1}{2})\ln(|A_1|)
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{laplaceH1evidence} separates the evidence into a best fit contribution, $\ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)$, and a local posterior volume contribution determined by $k_1$ and $|A_1|$.
The resolved EM data can alter both pieces: they may increase or decrease the joint peak according to GW-EM agreement and can substantially change the curvature in lens parameter directions. This is in the appendix along side applying it to the consistency integral to get Mahalanobis distance.

\section{$H_2$: catalogued lens without resolved strong lensing EM images}
$H_2$ retains the same common source lensing interpretation as $H_1$ but removes the strong lensing constraint.
The EM catalogue constrains the foreground galaxy itself through quantities such as $z_l,\sigma_v,q$ and photometry.
The parameter vector is correspondingly smaller,
\begin{equation}
\Theta_2 = \{\theta_{BBH},\theta_{lens},\beta,\nu_{EM}\}
\end{equation}
and the evidence is
\begin{equation}
Z_{2,c} = \int p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{lens},\beta,H_2)p(d_{EM,c}|\theta_{lens},\nu_{EM},H_2)\pi_2(\Theta_2)d\Theta_2
\end{equation}
The EM constrained lens posterior can again be propagated into $p(\psi|d_{EM},H_2)$, and its consistency with $p(\psi|d_{GW},H_2)$ can be measured by $C_2$.
Relative to $H_1$, the predictive distribution is generally expected to be broader because the gravitatioanl potential is constrained indirectly through galaxy properties rather than through a resolved image configuration.

\section{$H_3$: uncatalogued lens}
Under $H_3$ the GW pair is lensed, but the responsible foreground lens is absent from the catalogue.
Let
\begin{equation}
C_{cat}(\theta_{lens}) = P(\text{lens appears in catalogue}|\theta_{lens})
\end{equation}
so that $1-C_{cat}(\theta_{lens})$ is the probability that a lens with those properties is missed.
With
\begin{equation}
\Theta_3 = \{\theta_{BBH},\theta_{lens},\beta\}
\end{equation}
the evidence is
\begin{equation}
Z_3 = \int p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{lens},\beta,H_3)[1-C_{cat}(\theta_{lens})]\pi_{BBH}(\theta_{BBH})\pi_{lens,pop}(\theta_{lens})\pi_{\beta}(\beta)d\Theta_3
\end{equation}
$H_3$ therefore replaces a candidate specific EM likelihood with an astrophysical lens population prior weighted by catalogue non detection.
It would retained in the framework to ensure that the total lensed hypothesis does not implicitly assume that every physically relevant lens must already be catalogued.

\section{Laplace approximation to the full $H_1$ evidence}
Define the $H_1$ integrand
\begin{equation}
I_1(\Theta_1) = p(d_{GW}|\Theta_1,H_1)p(d_{EM,c}|\Theta_1,H_1)\pi_1(\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
Z_{1,c} = \int I_1(\Theta_1)d\Theta_1
\end{equation}
For Nnumerical stability, computations are performed in log space:
\begin{equation}
\ell_1(\Theta_1) = \ln(p(d_{GW}|\Theta_1,H_1))+\ln(p(d_{EM,c}|\Theta_1,H_1)+\ln\pi_1(\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{lnevidence}
Z_{1,c} = \int e^{[\ell_1(\Theta_1)]}d\Theta_1
\end{equation}
The maximum a posteriori point is
\begin{equation}
\hat\Theta_1 = \text{arg max}_{\Theta_1}\ell_1(\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
Near $\hat\Theta_1$, expand the log integrand to second order.
Since the gradient vanishes at an interior maximum, the local form is
\begin{equation}
\ell_1(\Theta_1) \approx \ell_1(\hat\Theta_1) - \frac{1}{2}(\Theta_1-\hat\Theta_1)^TA_1(\Theta_1-\hat\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
with negative Hessian
\begin{equation}
A_1 = -\nabla\nabla\ell_1(\Theta_1)|_{\hat\Theta_1}
\end{equation}
Substitutes into Eq.~\eqref{lnevidence} gives a multivariate Gaussian integral:
\begin{equation}
Z_{1,c} \approx e^{[\ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)]}(2\pi)^{\frac{k_1}{2}}|A_1|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
\end{equation}
where $k_1$ is the number of free continuous parameters.
In log form,
\begin{equation}
\ln(Z_{1,c}) \approx \ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)+(\frac{k_1}{2})\ln(2\pi)-(\frac{1}{2})\ln(|A_1|)
\end{equation}

\section{Gaussian approximation and Fisher matrix in the consistency statistic}
Recall the reduced consistency statistic
\begin{equation}
C_1 = \int \frac{p(\psi|d_{GW},H_1)p(\psi|d_{EM},H_1)}{\pi(\psi|H_1)} d\psi
\end{equation}
Posterior overlap statistics of this general form are widely used in Bayesian searches for strongly lensed GW event pairs \cite{22}, while later lensing frameworks perform more complete joint parameter inference and evidence calculations \cite{23}\cite{9}.\\
\\Assume that the GW and EM distributions are locally well approximated by $k_{\psi}$ dimensional Gaussian densities,
\begin{equation}
p(\psi|d_{GW},H_1) \simeq \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{GW},\Sigma_{GW})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
p(\psi|d_{EM},H_1) \simeq \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{EM},\Sigma_{EM})
\end{equation}
If the prior varies slowly across the region in which both distributions have appreciable support, it may be approximated locally by a constant $\pi(\psi|H_1) \simeq \pi_0$.
The consistency statistic then becomes
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{1}{\pi_0} \int \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{GW},\Sigma_{GW}) \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{EM},\Sigma_{EM}) d\psi
\end{equation}
The product integral of two normalised multivariate Gaussian densities is
\begin{equation}
\int \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_1,\Sigma_1) \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_2,\Sigma_2) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_1;\mu_2,\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2)
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{1}{\pi_0} \mathcal{N}(\mu_{GW};\mu_{EM},\Sigma_{GW}+\Sigma_{EM})
\end{equation}
Writing the Gaussian density explitly gives
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{e^{-\frac{D^2}{2}}}{\pi_0(2\pi)^{\frac{k_{\psi}}{2}}|\Sigma_{GW}+\Sigma_{EM}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
D^2=(\mu_{GW}-\mu_{EM})^T(\Sigma_{GW}+\Sigma_{EM})^{-1}(\mu_{GW}-\mu_{EM})
\end{equation}
This is the squared Mahalanobis distance \cite{24} between the two posterior means, evaluated usnig their combined covariance.\\
\\
Fisher information provides a complementary description of measurement precision.
For a likelihood $L(d|\psi)$ the expected Fisher matrix is defined by \cite{25}
\begin{equation}
F_{ij}=-\left\langle \frac{\partial^2 \ln L}{\partial \psi_i \partial \psi_j} \right\rangle
\end{equation}
under the usual regularity assumptions.
Near a well constrined maximum, the log likelihood may be expanded as
\begin{equation}
\ln(L(\psi)) \simeq \ln(L(\hat\psi))-\frac{1}{2}(\psi-\hat\psi)^TF(\psi-\hat\psi)
\end{equation}
In the asymptotic Gaussian limit, and when prior curvature is negligible,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma \simeq F^{-1}
\end{equation}
This approximation is standard in GW parameter estimation, but its domain of validity must be checked carefully: finite SNR ratio, weakly measured parameters, boundaries, degeneracies and informative priors can all make the Fisher approximation inaccurate \cite{26}.\\
\\
For the reduced consistency problem,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{GW} \simeq F_{GW}^{-1},    \Sigma_{EM} \simeq F_{EM}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The total covarience becomes
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{tot} \simeq F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus the Mahalanobis distance may be written directly in terms of the two information matrices,
\begin{equation}
D^2 = \Delta\mu^T(F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1})^{-1}\Delta\mu
\end{equation}
Substitution into the Gaussian overlap gives.
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\mu^T(F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1})^{-1}\Delta\mu}}{\pi_0(2\pi)^{\frac{k_{\psi}}{2}}|F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
\end{equation}
Taking the logartithm yields a numerically convenient decomposition,
\begin{equation}
\ln(C_1) \simeq -\ln(\pi_0)-\frac{k_{\psi}}{2}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(|F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}|)-\frac{1}{2}D^2
\end{equation}
The last two terms have distinct interpretations:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item $-\frac{D^2}{2}$ is the mismatch term. It penalises separation between the GW and EM predictions in units set by the combined uncertainty.
	\item $-\frac{1}{2}\ln(|F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}|)$ is the precision volume term. It increases as the joint uncertainty volume becomes smaller.
\end{enumerate}
This decomposition makes clear why resolved strong lensing EM information can be highly discriminating under $H_1$.
If the EM lens reconstruction is precise, $F_{EM}$ has large eigenvalues and $F_{EM}^{-1}$ is small.
Agreement with the GW posterior can then produce a sharply concentrated overlap.
Conversely, a precise EM prediction that is offset from the GW inference can make $D^2$ large and suppress $C_1$ exponentially.
