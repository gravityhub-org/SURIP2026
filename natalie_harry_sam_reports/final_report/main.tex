\section*{Abstract}
%sam did this
Strong gravitational lensing of gravitational waves is an expected consequence of the propagation of compact binary signals through an inhomogeneous universe, and a small but non-zero fraction of events detected by ground based observatories should be multiply imaged by intervening galaxies or clusters \cite{2}\cite{6}\cite{18}\cite{19}.
Recent forecasts indicate that the first confident strongly lensed gravitational wave detection could occur during the fifth LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA observing run (O5)\cite{19}, while searches incorporating O1-O4a events forecast a substantial probability of reaching at least $3\sigma$ significance in O5\cite{20}.
At the same time, simulations tailored to the wide field capabilities of the Euclid telescope demonstrate that high resolution imaging can successfully indentify and localize the strongly lensed host galaxies of merging binary black holes, significantly narrowing down the gravitatioanl wave sky localization area \cite{27}.
Motivated by these results, this work develops a Bayesian framework in which a catalogued lens with resolved strong lensing information is used to increase the confidence of a candidate lensed GW pair.
We derive the full resolved strong lensing evidence, reconstruct the binary black hole population for prior odds, gravitational wave only bayes factor and joint analysis of the evidence, producing a joint bayes factor. The remaining hypotheses describe a catalogued lens without resolved images, an uncatalogued lens and two independent unlensed binary black holes.
The central forecast to be tested is that targeted (EM) follow-up can, for favourable resolved strong lensing systems, sharpen the lens prediction sufficiently to raise the strong lensing detection confidence toward $5\sigma$.\\
Keywords: gravitational waves; gravitational lensing: strong; Bayesian inference; electromagnetic catalogues; binary black holes; multi-messenger astronomy.\\

\section{Introduction}
%sam did this
Gravitational waves propagate along null geodesics and are therefore subject to gravitational lensing in the same geometric-optics sense as electromagnetic radiation \cite{2}\cite{1}. 
For galaxy-scale lenses, a single compact-binary coalescence can produce multiple detectable images separated by minutes to months, depending on the lens potential and source geometry.
To leading order, the images share the same intrinsic binary parameters while differing in arrival time, amplitude through the lensing magnification and an image-type-dependent phase contribution \cite{6}\cite{3}\cite{4}\cite{5}.
This repeated signal structure motivates searches for pairs or multiples of GW events that are mutually consistent under a common source hypothesis.\\
\\The central statistical difficulty is that an unlensed population can also produce event pairs with overlapping posterior distributions. 
A robust lensing claim must therefore compare a common-source model against a model in which the detections arise from independent binaries.
Bayesian model selection provides a natural framework because it combines likelihood agreement, prior information, population expectations and parameter-space volume into a single evidence ratio \cite{7}\cite{8}\cite{9}\cite{10}.
Searches of LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA data have so far found no compelling population level evidence for strong lensing, reinforcing the need for careful prior odds and follow up tests \cite{12}\cite{13}\cite{14}.\\
\\A major source of additional information is the electromagnetic foreground. 
A galaxy or cluster capable of producing strong lensing may be visible in imaging or spectroscopic catalogues even when the BBH source itself has no detectable EM counterpart.
The available EM information can range from simple measurements of the foreground galaxy to a resolved strong-lensing system containing arcs, multiple images or an Einstein ring from an unrelated background source.
These two types of information constrain different parts of the lens model.
The foreground galaxy light constrains the luminous structure and population properties of the deflector, while resolved lensed source light constrains the mapping produced by the lens mass distribution.\\
\\For this reason, the parameterisation adopted here separates the EM lens model into source light parameters, lens light parameters and lens mass parameters.
This is preferable to grouping all EM quantities into a single generic lens vector because it makes clear which parameter blocks are directly measured and which are only inferred indirectly under each hypothesis.

\section{Bayesian hypothesis space}
%sams part
Consider two GW detections, $d_{GW}={d_{GW,1},d_{GW,2}}$, together with EM observations dEM drawin from a catalogue of potential foreground lenses. 
We divide the lensing interpretation into three mutually exclusive observational cases and compare their union with an unlensed alternative.
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Mutually exclusive hypotheses used in the joint GW-EM model comparison}
\label{}
\begin{tabular}{|p{2cm}|p{3cm}|p{4cm}|}
\hline
Hypothesis & GW interpretation & EM/catalogue information \\ \hline
$H_1$       & Two detections are lensed images of the same BBH.        & The repsonsible lens is catalogues and resolved strong-lensing EM information constrains a detailed lens model.         \\ \hline
$H_2$       & Two detections are lensed images of the same BBH.        & The responsible lens is catalogues, but no resolved strong-lensing image configuration is available; EM data constrain galaxy properties.        \\ \hline
$H_3$       & Two detections are lensed images of the same BBH.        & The responsible foreground lens is absent from the catalogue.              \\ \hline
$H_U$       & The detections arise from two independent unlensed BBHs.         & Any galaxy in the search region is treated as an unrelated field object, conditional on the search selection.              \\ \hline  
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
\begin{equation}
H_L = H_1 \cup H_2 \cup H_3
\end{equation}
with $H_1$, $H_2$ and $H_3$ mutually exclusive within the assumed search space. 
The posterior probability of the total lensed hypothesis is therefore
\begin{equation}
p(H_L|d) = p(H_1|d) + p(H_2|d) + p(H_3|d)
\end{equation}
where $d={d_{GW},d_{EM}}$. For each model $H_i$, Bayes' theorem gives
\begin{equation}
p(H_i|d) = Z_i \frac{p(H_i)}{p(d)}, Z_i \equiv p(d|H_i).
\end{equation}
The posterior odds of lensing relative to the unlsened hypothesis are consequently
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:posteriorodds}
\frac{p(H_L|d)}{p(H_U|d)} = \frac{[Z_1p(H_1) + Z_2p(H_2) + Z_3p(H_3)]}{Z_Up(H_U)}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:posteriorodds} makes the role of the hypothesis prior explicit. 
The Bayes factor quantifies how the data update the relative support for the models, while the prior odds encode the expected rarity of strong lensing and the probability that a responsible lens belongs to each catalogue class. 
This distinction is important because prior odds and search selection can change substantially as the GW catalogue grows \cite{7}\cite{9}\cite{14}.

\section{Parameterisation and Conditional Structure}

\subsection{Binary Black Hole Source Parameters}

Let $\theta_{BBH}$ denote the physical parameters of the compact binary source.
A representative set is
\begin{equation}
\theta_{BBH}=\{m_1,m_2,\chi_1,\chi_2,z_s,\Omega_s,\iota,\phi_{pol},...\}.
\end{equation}
Under any lensed hypothesis, the intrinsic source parameters are shared by the images, whereas under $H_U$ the two detections have independent parameter sets $\theta_{BBH,1}$ and $\theta_{BBH,2}$.
Apparent luminosity distances are image dependent because magnification rescales the observed strain amplitude.

\subsection{Source light parameters}

The source light block describes the resolved electromagnetic background source whose images or arcs are used to constrain the foreground lens.
It does not represent an electromagnetic counterpart to the BBH.
A generic source light parameter vector may be written
\begin{equation}
\theta_{SL} = \{\Omega_{s,EM},F_s,R_{eff,s},n_s,q_s,\phi_s,colour/SED_s,...\}
\end{equation}
Here $\Omega_{s,EM}$ is the unlensed source-plane centroid, $F_s$ is the source flux or surface-brightness normalisation, $R_{eff,s}$ is an effective radius, $n_s$ is a Sersic indec, $q_s$ is the projected axis ratio and $\psi_s$ is the source orientation.
A pixelised or basis function source reconstruction may be used instead, in that case $\theta_{SL}$ represents the corresponding source light coefficients and regularisation hyperparameters.\\
\\The source light is required only when the resolved strong lensing EM data are modelled (under $H_1$).
It is absent from $H_2$ because no resolved lensed source is availible, and absent from $H_3$ because no foreground lens candidate is indentified.

\subsection{Lens light parameters}

The lens light block describes the luminous distribution of the foreground deflector and the observables that determine whether it is present in the catalogue.
A representative parameter vector is
\begin{equation}
\theta_{LL} = \{\Omega_l,F_l,R_{eff,l},n_l,q_l,\psi_l,colour/SED_l,z_l,...\}
\end{equation}
These parameters describe the centroid, brightness, size, Sersic profile, axis ratio, position angle, spectral energy distribution information and redshift of the foregoround galaxy.
Depending on the data set, the parameters may also contain velocity dispersion measurements or population level nuisance quantities associated with photomoetric redshift and stellar population inference.\\
\\Under $H_1$ and $H_2$, $\theta_{LL}$ is constrained directly by the catalogue or imaging data.
Under $H_3$, $\theta_{LL}$ is not observed but can be retained as a latent population variable because catalogue completeness depends primarily on the brightness, size, redshift and surface brightness properties of the foregrouhd galaxy.

\subsection{Lens mass parameters}

The lens mass block describes the gravitational potential responsible for producing the multiple GW images.
A model dependent vector may be written
\begin{equation}
\theta_{LM} = \{\theta_E \text{or} \sigma_v,\gamma^{'},q_m,\phi_m,\Omega_m,\gamma_{ext},\phi_{ext},\kappa_{ext},...\}
\end{equation}
Here $\theta_E \text{or} \sigma_v$ sets the mass scale, $\gamma^{'}$ is a density slope parameter where relevant, $q_m \text{and} \phi_m$ describe a projected mass ellipticity and orientation, $\Omega_m$ is the mass centroid, and $\gamma_{ext}, \phi_{ext} \text{and} \kappa_{ext}$ describe line of sight or enviornmental perturbations.
The precise contents depend on whether the adopted model is, for example, a singular isothermal ellipsoid, an elliptical power law mass distribution or a more flexible composite model.\\
\\The lens mass parameters are the central bridge between GW and EM analyses.
The GW pair constrains combination of $\theta_{LM}$ through the image relations, while the EM data constrain $\theta_{LM}$ either directly through resolved strong lensing morphology under $H_1$ or indirectly through tis relation to $\theta_{LL}$ under $H_2$.

\subsection{Relation between the parameter blocks}

The lens model parameter space is therefore organised as
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{lens model} = \{\theta_{SL},\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM},\nu_{EM}\}
\end{equation}
with the most important caveat that the available blocks depend on the hypothesis. 
The lens light and lens mass blocks need not be statistically independent.
A physically motivated population prior may instead take the form
\begin{equation}
\pi(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})=\pi(\theta_{LM}|\theta_{LL})\pi(\theta_{LL})
\end{equation}
or the reverse conditioning, depending on how stellar light, velocity dispersion, halo mass and mass to light relations are modelled.
Treating this correlation explicitly is especially important under $H_2$, where the lens light supplies the principle EM route to constraining lens mass.

\subsection{Image Level Lensing Observables}

The GW pair constrains a lower dimensional set of relations between the images,
\begin{equation}
\psi_{GW}=\{\Delta t, \mu_r, \Delta n, \Omega, ...\}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t$ is the relative arrive time delay, $\mu_r$ is a relative magnification ratio, $\Delta n$ denotes the relative Morse index, and $\Omega$ represents common sky localisation.
These quantities are generated by the lens equation and Fermat potenital rather than being independent parameters,
\begin{equation}
\psi=f(\theta_{LM},z_s,\beta;cosmology).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Electromagnetic Nuisance Parameters}

EM observations require nuisance parameters $\nu_{EM}$ describing measurement and modelling uncertainties.
Depending on the catalogue and imaging data, $\nu_{EM}$ may include the point spread function, photometruc calibration, specral energy distribution parameters, stellar mass to light ratio, background subtraction, photometric redshift calibration and other instrument or population level terms.
These parameter should be maginalised rather than fixed when their uncertainty is non negligible, beause overly narrow EM constraints can artificially increase the GW-EM consistency factor.

\section{Unlensed Hypothesis}
%sam
Under $H_U$ the detections correspond to two independent BBH mergers.
The GW evidence is
\begin{equation}
Z_{GW,U} = \int p(d_{GW,1}|\theta_{BBH,1},H_U) p(d_{GW,2}|\theta_{BBH,2},H_U) \pi_U(\theta_{BBH,1},\theta_{BBH,2})d\theta_{BBH,1}d\theta_{BBH,2}
\end{equation}
The EM observations must not be allowed to depend pn whether the GW pair happens to be unlensed.
We therefore introduce an EM field model, $H_{EM,field}$, that describes unrelated foreground galaxies under the same catalogue/search selection.
When the GW and EM data are conditionally independent under $H_U$,
\begin{equation}
H_U \equiv H_{GW,U} \cap H_{EM,field}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
Z_U = Z_{GW,U} Z_{EM,field}, Z_{EM,field} = p(d_{EM}|H_{EM,field})
\end{equation}
This term is essential whenever the lensed hypotheses recieve evidence from the existence or properties of a catalogue galaxy.
Omitting the corresponding field galaxy probability from $H_U$ would systematically favour catalogues lens hypotheses simply because a galaxy was searched for and found.

\subsection{Full joint evidence and prior}
%sam
\begin{equation}
p(d_{GW},d_{EM,c}|\Theta_1,H_1) = p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{LM},\beta_{GW},H_1)p(d_{EM,c}|\theta_{SL},\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM},\nu_{EM},H_1).
\end{equation}
The full evidence for candidate c is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H1evidence}
Z_{1,c} = \int p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{LM},\beta_{GW},H_1)p(d_{EM,c}|\theta_{SL},\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM},\nu_{EM},H_1)\pi_1(\Theta_1)d\Theta_1.
\end{equation}
A useful schematic prior factorisation is
\begin{equation}
\pi_1(\Theta_1)=\pi_{BBH}\pi_{\beta_{GW}}\pi_{SL}\pi(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})\pi_{\nu_{EM}}S_1.
\end{equation}
The joint prior $\pi(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})$ allows correlations between galaxy light and mass to be retained.
$S_1$ denotes the required GW detection, catalogue and resolved lens selection conditioning.
 The evidence intergrated over both the scientifically interesting lens mass parameters and the source light, lens light and nuisance parameters required by the EM reconstruction.

\subsection{GW observables and the EM predictive distribution}

The resolved EM data determine a posterior for the lens mass after marginalising over source light, lens light and EM nuisance parameters:
\begin{equation}
p(\theta_{LM}|d_{EM,c},H_1) \propto \int p(d_{EM,c}|\theta_{SL},\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM},\nu_{EM},H_1)\pi_1d\theta_{SL}d\theta_{LL}d\nu_{EM}
\end{equation}
Propagating this mass posterior through the lens equation gives the predictive distribution
\begin{equation}
p(\psi|d_{EM,c},H_1) = \int p(\psi|\theta_{LM},z_s,\beta_{GW},H_1)p(\theta_{LM},z_s,\beta_{GW}|d_{EM,c},H_1)d\theta_{LM}dz_sd\beta_{GW}
\end{equation}
The corresponding prior corrected consistency statistic is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H1consistency}
C_1 = \int \frac{p(\psi|d_{GW}H_1)p(\psi|d_{EM,c},H_1)}{\pi(\psi|H_1)}d\psi
\end{equation}
A large $C_1$ indicates that the resolved EM reconstruction predicts time delays, magnification relations and image properties compatible with the GW pair.
A small value indicates tension.
When the full evidence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H1evidence} is evaluated directly, this consistency is already encoded through $\theta_{LM}$; $C_1$ is therefore most safely interpreted as a reduced space diagnostic unless an explicit non overlapping factorisation is derived.

\subsection{Multiple resolved lens candidates}

If more than one resolved strong lensing system is compatiable with the GW localisation, the lens indentity should be marginalised rather than fixed in advance:
\begin{equation}
Z_1 = \sum_{c=1}^{N_{cat}}p(c|H_1)Z_{1,c}
\end{equation}

\section{Reduced GW-EM consistency under $H_1$}

The reduced consistency statistic isolates the direct comparison between the GW inferred image relations and the prediction from the resolved EM lens reconstruction.
Its prior denominator removes the common prior contribution that would otherwise be counted twice when multiplying posteriors.\\
\\In the present parameterisation, the key reaon $H_1$ can be highly constraining is that the resolved source light morphology $\theta_{SL}$ is lensed by the same $\theta_{LM}$ that generates the GW image relations.
Marginalising over $\theta_{SL}$ and $\theta_{LL}$ can still leave a comparatively narrow posterior on $\theta_{LM}$, provided the strong lensing configuration is informative.

\section{Computational evaluation of $H_1$}

The integrals can be manipulated and approximated using laplace to get a form more suitable for computational analysis.
Using the joint maximum and laplace approximation the full evidence for $H_1$ can be written, in log form as,
\begin{equation}
\label{laplaceH1evidence}
\ln(Z_{1,c}) \approx \ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)+(\frac{k_1}{2})\ln(2\pi)-(\frac{1}{2})\ln(|A_1|)
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{laplaceH1evidence} separates the evidence into a best fit contribution, $\ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)$, and a local posterior volume contribution determined by $k_1$ and $|A_1|$.
The resolved EM data can alter both the peak term and the local posterior volume.
In the source light/lens light/lens mass parameterisation, the full Hessian contains cross block curvature.
In particular, degeneracies between source light and lens mass, and between lens light and lens mass, should be retained rather tha ignored by approximating the blocks independently.

\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\ln(Z_{2,c}) \simeq \ell_2(\hat\Theta_2)+(\frac{k_2}{2})\ln(2\pi)-(\frac{1}{2}\ln(|A_2|)
\end{equation}
The curvature matrix $A_2$ contains cross information between $\theta_{LL} \text{and} \theta_{LM}$ through their population relation.
Because no $\theta_{SL}$ block is present, the EM data does not provide the strong image geometry constraints available under $H_1$.
The resulting posterior in the lens mass directions will therefore normally be broader and more prior dependent.\\
\\
\textbf{Restriction of $H_2$}\\
\\
$H_2$ is restricted to systems in which the responsible lens is present in the catalogue but no resolved strong lensing EM configuration is used.
If a resolved lensed source is available, the system belongs ot $H_1$, if the responsible foreground lens is absent from the catalogue, it belongs to $H_3$.\\
\\
The principle physical limitation is that lens light does not uniquely determine lens mass.
The relation $\pi(\theta_{LM}|\theta_{LL})$ must therefore include intrinsic scatter and relevent uncertainties.
Treating the lens light measurement as a deteministic mass estimate would artificially narrow the $H_2$ evidence.\\
\\
The source redishift and GW source plane position remain latent unless measure independetly.
The Laplace approximation also requires an approximately single mode, locally Gaussian posterior.
Weak lens mass constraints and light to mass degeneracies can make this assumption poor.

\section{$H_3$: uncatalogued lens}

\textbf{Latent lens light and lens mass population}\\
\\
Under $H_3$, the GW pair is lensed but the responsible foreground lens is not present in the catalogue.
No condidate specific light source or lens light likelihood exists.
Nevertheless, retaining a latent $\theta_{LL}$ bloack is usedful because catalogue detection depends on the luminous properties of the deflector, while the lensing signal depends on $\theta_{LM}$.
\begin{equation}
\Theta_3=\{\theta_{BBH},\beta_{GW},\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM}\}
\end{equation}
The catalogue completeness function is written
\begin{equation}
C_{cat}(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM} = P(\text{lens appears in catalogue}|\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})
\end{equation}
where dependence on $\theta_{LM}$ may be retained when survey selection is correlated with mass through velocity disperiosn, halo mass or enviromental properties.
In the simplest implementation the completenedd may depend only on the observable lens light block.
\\
\\
\textbf{Full evidence}\\
\\
The $H_3$ evidence becomes
\begin{equation}
Z_3 = \int p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{LM},\beta_{GW},H_3)[1-C_{cat}(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})]\pi_{BBH}\pi_{\beta_{GW}}\pi(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})d\Theta_3
\end{equation}
This form makes the parameter space structure explicit.
The GW likelihood selects lens mass configurations capable of producing the observed image relations, the joint lens light.lens mass population prior supplies physically plausiable deflectors, and the non-detection factor supresses systems whose light properties imply that they should have appeared in the catalogue.
\\
\\
\textbf{Log integrand and laplace approximation}\\
\\
\begin{equation}
\ell_3(\Theta_3) = \ln(p(d_{GW}|\theta_{BBH},\theta_{LM},\beta_{GW},H_3)) + \ln([1-C_{cat}(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})]) + \ln(\pi_{BBH}) + \ln(\pi_{\beta_{GW}}) + \ln(\pi(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM}))
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\hat\Theta_3 = \text{arg max}_{\Theta_3}\ell_3(\Theta_3)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
A_3 = -\nabla\nabla\ell_3(\Theta_3)|_{\hat\Theta_3}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\ln(Z_3) \simeq \ell_3(\hat\Theta_3) + (\frac{k_3}{2})\ln(2\pi) - (\frac{1}{2})\ln(|A_3|)
\end{equation}
The catalogue non-detection term can alter both the height of the dominant mode and the curvature of the posterior.
A lens mass solution that fits the GW well can still be suppressed if its correlated lens light properties make non-detection improbable.
\\
\\
\textbf{Why there is no source light consistency factor under $H_3$}\\
\\
There is no condidate specific resolved background source under $H_3$, and therefore no surce light block $\theta_{SL}$ and no EM reconstruction from which to contrsuct $p(\psi|d_{EM,c},H_3)$.
The role played by $C_1 \text{and} C_2$ is replaced by marginalisation over the latent lens light/lens mass population and the catalogue non-detection probability.
\\
\\
\textbf{Restriction of $H_3$}\\
\\
$H_3$ applies only when the responsible lens is absent from the adopted catalogue.
The completeness model must therefore refer to exactly the same survery footprint, depth and object selection criteria used to define $H_1 \text{and} H_2$.\\
\\
The joint population model $\pi(\theta_{LL},\theta_{LM})$ is essential.
If lens light is omitted completely, the catalogue non-detection probability must be expressed indirectly as a function of mass, which can hide substantial astrophyscial uncertainty.
Retaining $\theta_{LL}$ as a latent variable makes the selection model more transparent.\\
\\
The laplace approximation can fail when catalogue completeness changes sharply near a survey threshold, when the light to mass population relation is strongly non-Gaussian or when several lens mass configurations provide comparable fits to the GW data.

\section{Laplace approximation to the full $H_1$ evidence}
Define the $H_1$ integrand
\begin{equation}
I_1(\Theta_1) = p(d_{GW}|\Theta_1,H_1)p(d_{EM,c}|\Theta_1,H_1)\pi_1(\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
Z_{1,c} = \int I_1(\Theta_1)d\Theta_1
\end{equation}
For Nnumerical stability, computations are performed in log space:
\begin{equation}
\ell_1(\Theta_1) = \ln(p(d_{GW}|\Theta_1,H_1))+\ln(p(d_{EM,c}|\Theta_1,H_1)+\ln\pi_1(\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{lnevidence}
Z_{1,c} = \int e^{[\ell_1(\Theta_1)]}d\Theta_1
\end{equation}
The maximum a posteriori point is
\begin{equation}
\hat\Theta_1 = \text{arg max}_{\Theta_1}\ell_1(\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
Near $\hat\Theta_1$, expand the log integrand to second order.
Since the gradient vanishes at an interior maximum, the local form is
\begin{equation}
\ell_1(\Theta_1) \approx \ell_1(\hat\Theta_1) - \frac{1}{2}(\Theta_1-\hat\Theta_1)^TA_1(\Theta_1-\hat\Theta_1)
\end{equation}
with negative Hessian
\begin{equation}
A_1 = -\nabla\nabla\ell_1(\Theta_1)|_{\hat\Theta_1}
\end{equation}
Substitutes into Eq.~\eqref{lnevidence} gives a multivariate Gaussian integral:
\begin{equation}
Z_{1,c} \approx e^{[\ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)]}(2\pi)^{\frac{k_1}{2}}|A_1|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
\end{equation}
where $k_1$ is the number of free continuous parameters.
In log form,
\begin{equation}
\ln(Z_{1,c}) \approx \ell_1(\hat\Theta_1)+(\frac{k_1}{2})\ln(2\pi)-(\frac{1}{2})\ln(|A_1|)
\end{equation}

\section{Gaussian approximation and Fisher matrix in the consistency statistic}
Recall the reduced consistency statistic
\begin{equation}
C_1 = \int \frac{p(\psi|d_{GW},H_1)p(\psi|d_{EM},H_1)}{\pi(\psi|H_1)} d\psi
\end{equation}
Posterior overlap statistics of this general form are widely used in Bayesian searches for strongly lensed GW event pairs \cite{22}, while later lensing frameworks perform more complete joint parameter inference and evidence calculations \cite{23}\cite{9}.\\
\\Assume that the GW and EM distributions are locally well approximated by $k_{\psi}$ dimensional Gaussian densities,
\begin{equation}
p(\psi|d_{GW},H_1) \simeq \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{GW},\Sigma_{GW})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
p(\psi|d_{EM},H_1) \simeq \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{EM},\Sigma_{EM})
\end{equation}
If the prior varies slowly across the region in which both distributions have appreciable support, it may be approximated locally by a constant $\pi(\psi|H_1) \simeq \pi_0$.
The consistency statistic then becomes
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{1}{\pi_0} \int \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{GW},\Sigma_{GW}) \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_{EM},\Sigma_{EM}) d\psi
\end{equation}
The product integral of two normalised multivariate Gaussian densities is
\begin{equation}
\int \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_1,\Sigma_1) \mathcal{N}(\psi;\mu_2,\Sigma_2) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_1;\mu_2,\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2)
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{1}{\pi_0} \mathcal{N}(\mu_{GW};\mu_{EM},\Sigma_{GW}+\Sigma_{EM})
\end{equation}
Writing the Gaussian density explitly gives
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{e^{-\frac{D^2}{2}}}{\pi_0(2\pi)^{\frac{k_{\psi}}{2}}|\Sigma_{GW}+\Sigma_{EM}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
D^2=(\mu_{GW}-\mu_{EM})^T(\Sigma_{GW}+\Sigma_{EM})^{-1}(\mu_{GW}-\mu_{EM})
\end{equation}
This is the squared Mahalanobis distance \cite{24} between the two posterior means, evaluated usnig their combined covariance.\\
\\
Fisher information provides a complementary description of measurement precision.
For a likelihood $L(d|\psi)$ the expected Fisher matrix is defined by \cite{25}
\begin{equation}
F_{ij}=-\left\langle \frac{\partial^2 \ln L}{\partial \psi_i \partial \psi_j} \right\rangle
\end{equation}
under the usual regularity assumptions.
Near a well constrined maximum, the log likelihood may be expanded as
\begin{equation}
\ln(L(\psi)) \simeq \ln(L(\hat\psi))-\frac{1}{2}(\psi-\hat\psi)^TF(\psi-\hat\psi)
\end{equation}
In the asymptotic Gaussian limit, and when prior curvature is negligible,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma \simeq F^{-1}
\end{equation}
This approximation is standard in GW parameter estimation, but its domain of validity must be checked carefully: finite SNR ratio, weakly measured parameters, boundaries, degeneracies and informative priors can all make the Fisher approximation inaccurate \cite{26}.\\
\\
For the reduced consistency problem,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{GW} \simeq F_{GW}^{-1},    \Sigma_{EM} \simeq F_{EM}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The total covarience becomes
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{tot} \simeq F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus the Mahalanobis distance may be written directly in terms of the two information matrices,
\begin{equation}
D^2 = \Delta\mu^T(F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1})^{-1}\Delta\mu
\end{equation}
Substitution into the Gaussian overlap gives.
\begin{equation}
C_1 \simeq \frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\mu^T(F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1})^{-1}\Delta\mu}}{\pi_0(2\pi)^{\frac{k_{\psi}}{2}}|F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
\end{equation}
Taking the logartithm yields a numerically convenient decomposition,
\begin{equation}
\ln(C_1) \simeq -\ln(\pi_0)-\frac{k_{\psi}}{2}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(|F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}|)-\frac{1}{2}D^2
\end{equation}
The last two terms have distinct interpretations:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item $-\frac{D^2}{2}$ is the mismatch term. It penalises separation between the GW and EM predictions in units set by the combined uncertainty.
	\item $-\frac{1}{2}\ln(|F_{GW}^{-1}+F_{EM}^{-1}|)$ is the precision volume term. It increases as the joint uncertainty volume becomes smaller.
\end{enumerate}
This decomposition makes clear why resolved strong lensing EM information can be highly discriminating under $H_1$.
If the EM lens reconstruction is precise, $F_{EM}$ has large eigenvalues and $F_{EM}^{-1}$ is small.
Agreement with the GW posterior can then produce a sharply concentrated overlap.
Conversely, a precise EM prediction that is offset from the GW inference can make $D^2$ large and suppress $C_1$ exponentially.
